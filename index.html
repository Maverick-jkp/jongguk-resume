Origin
“Take something complex. Understand it deeply. Then explain it so clearly that someone else can act on it.”

I started my career in Tokyo, teaching Korean to Japanese students. The company’s textbooks were too formal for real conversation, so I rewrote them — breaking down grammar from its etymological roots, building structural explanations that made the language click. Within months I was recognized as the top instructor across all NOVA branches in Japan.

That experience gave me a habit I still rely on: when something is complex, I don’t simplify it — I find the right angle to make it clear. Whether it’s explaining a product’s value to a Japanese enterprise client, translating a technical requirement for a BD team, or building a Slack bot that lets non-technical colleagues query data in plain language — the work is the same.

That instinct took me from language education to Google Play Games, then to Rakuten, ProtoPie, and now Xsolla — always at the intersection where something technical needs to become something human.
