Origin
“Take something complex. Understand it deeply. Then explain it so clearly that someone else can act on it.”

I started my career in Tokyo, teaching Korean to Japanese students. The company’s textbooks were too formal for real conversation, so I rewrote them — breaking down grammar from its etymological roots, building structural explanations that made the language click. Within months I was recognized as the top instructor across all NOVA branches in Japan.

That experience gave me a habit I still rely on: when something is complex, I don’t simplify it — I find the right angle to make it clear. Whether it’s explaining a product’s value to a Japanese enterprise client, translating a technical requirement for a BD team, or building a Slack bot that lets non-technical colleagues query data in plain language — the work is the same.

That instinct took me from language education to Google Play Games, then to Rakuten, ProtoPie, and now Xsolla — always at the intersection where something technical needs to become something human.

Now

At Xsolla I run regional product work for 20+ APAC enterprise gaming partners, single point of contact from pre-sales through adoption and upsell. The work I’m closest to right now is a usability initiative on our customer dashboard. Data showed several features were underused, so I’m running onboarding tutorial additions, UX writing simplification, flow shortening, and an interactive demo for sales enablement, all in parallel.

I also build. The team I’m on doesn’t have a dedicated regional engineer, so when something needs to ship faster than a sprint, I tend to write it myself. Over the past year I shipped four production tools and 20+ PRs as a non-engineer, working with Claude and Gemini through their APIs on Cloud Run. Project gallery is below.

Before Xsolla, the most consequential work I did was at Send Anywhere, where I led the transition from a near-zero-revenue model (a cheap lifetime ad-removal IAP) to an ads-and-subscription model. The negotiation with AppLovin and Unity Ads was the easy part. The interesting part was the segmentation that came first: cutting users by usage frequency, feature mix, and OS pairs to find the cross-OS large-file moments where ads would actually be tolerated. Internal pushback was about app-store ratings, so I wrote the hypothesis down upfront and instrumented churn and rating to validate. Neither moved.

On the same product I built the web-to-app conversion campaigns that pushed users from the lower-LTV web channel into mobile, and redesigned the entire checkout flow after funnel analysis showed where users were dropping. On Rakuten Drive, I drove a full app redesign through A/B testing on the initial screen (gallery view vs the folder-tree convention users expected from drive apps), validated post-launch with NPS lift.
